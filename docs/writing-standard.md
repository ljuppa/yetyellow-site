# Writing standard

Apply this standard to every website text and description.

## Aim

Write from observed work, specific judgement and real constraints. Do not try to make copy appear human by adding informality. The test is whether the text says something that could only credibly come from this person and this work.

Write in clear international English with a Dutch professional register. The author is European and lives in the Netherlands. The writing should be direct, sober and precise, without attempting American promotional language or colloquial native-English ease.

## Rules

- State the activity, decision, constraint or problem before claiming an outcome.
- Use first person when the work, judgement or responsibility belongs to the author.
- Prefer plain verbs: lead, test, build, assess, decide, publish.
- Use short, straightforward sentence structures. Prefer familiar words over idiom, clever phrasing or cultural shorthand.
- Use British spelling and punctuation conventions.
- Name trade-offs, limits and unresolved points when they are relevant.
- Use a claim only when it is supported by evidence or can be made specific.
- Remove any sentence that would work equally well on another leader's or consultancy's website.

## Avoid

- Abstract claims such as "turning ambition into adopted practice" without concrete context.
- Artificial three-part phrasing and symmetrical sentence structures.
- Formulaic contrasts such as "not X, but Y" and "X rather than Y".
- Generic linking phrases such as "at the intersection of", "reflecting", "highlighting" and "underscoring".
- Inflated claims of significance, impact, leadership or value that the text does not demonstrate.
- Marketing language and generic AI vocabulary used as a substitute for a point of view.
- American corporate language, hype, informal self-promotion, rhetorical flourish and forced conversational warmth.

## Final check

Before approving copy, ask:

1. What real work or judgement does this sentence refer to?
2. Can a reader see what is being claimed without accepting an assertion on trust?
3. Would this still fit any other AI advisory website? If yes, rewrite or remove it.
